## Executive finding

The current architecture has **two Horizon supervisors on one worker service**, not one shared pool: `default` has at most 3 processes and `extraction` defaults to 2. The extraction queue is nevertheless a catch-all for chunk extraction, legacy AI work, text extraction, OCR, merge, synthesis, comparisons, visuals, insights, and embeddings. Its two processes are the *configured-default* bound for jobs on that queue on the current single worker replica; the effective production environment override was not read. A document may consume both when the default applies. A merge or summary may wait behind many long extraction jobs. The per-document setting bounds *outstanding dispatched chunks*, not fair worker allocation or all Anthropic requests. Production now places the web and worker in **US West (`us-west2`)**, with Postgres and Redis in **San Francisco (`sfo`)**; the prompt's Virginia placement is historical. Railway's region names differ, so verify latency rather than assuming zero distance, but the present layout no longer shows a Virginia-to-California hop.

The smallest useful next topology is **`extraction`, `synthesis`, and `default`** with independent worker capacity, plus one shared, atomic Redis provider admission limit. Keep merge with synthesis initially, because it is a prerequisite for synthesis and has bounded current volume; measure merge and synthesis wait before considering a fourth pool. Keep per-document fan-out at 2 initially. Raise extraction capacity in a measured 2 → 4 → 6 → 8 sequence only after account limits, worker memory, and all call paths are accounted for. This is an implementation recommendation, not a configuration change made by this audit.

## 3. Fairness and simultaneous-document model

`IncrementalPipeline::pump()` locks the document row in a Postgres transaction, counts extraction chunks with `queued` or `running` status for that document and pipeline, and dispatches up to `DOCINTEL_EXTRACTION_CONCURRENCY` outstanding chunks after commit. This is race safe for concurrent pumps of the **same document**. Chunk execution locks the document and chunk rows before reserving cost and changing status to `running`. Duplicate delivery of a completed/running chunk returns before the billable extraction call, although the free `countTokens()` call can occur before the claim for a `queued` duplicate. The per-document limit does **not** apply to legacy four-job batches, OCR, insights, summaries, comparisons, context calls, or synchronous Q&A. It is not an active-call semaphore.

At checked-in defaults (`per-document=2`, **one** worker replica, extraction workers=2), the models below assume five incremental roots/document, successful calls of similar duration, no OCR, splits, legacy jobs, or other queue traffic. `min(2, 2 × documents)` is the maximum running **chunk jobs**, but all `extraction` jobs share the same two processes, so actual running chunks can be lower. If production sets a different environment override, substitute that global process count; the effective value was not verified.

| Scenario | Initial queued chunks | Simultaneous chunks | Likely queue behavior |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| A. 1 document, 5 roots | 2 | 2 | Both workers may serve it; the remaining 3 roots are pumped as slots free. |
| B. 2 documents, 5 roots each | 4 | 2 total, 0–2 each | If A's two jobs enqueue first, A occupies both until a job finishes. B eventually runs under ordinary queue ordering, with no reserved share. |
| C. 5 documents, 5 roots each | 10 | 2 total, at most 2/document | At least 3 documents wait with no running chunk initially. Initial FIFO ordering reflects dispatch order, not document fairness. |
| D. 20 documents within one minute | 40 | 2 total | Up to 38 initial chunk jobs wait, plus later roots/splits/merge/synthesis. Queue wait and completion time grow with offered load; no admission/backpressure prevents unbounded accumulation. |

At 10 simultaneous documents, up to 20 initial chunks are queued and 2 run; at 5 and 20, up to 10 and 40 respectively. These are concurrency and queue counts, **not throughput or wait-time forecasts**. A third document can progress only after a worker reaches one of its queued jobs. FIFO-ish Redis ordering prevents a single document from permanently jumping ahead with every newly pumped child, because new jobs append behind jobs already ready; delayed retries enter later. But there is no round-robin scheduler or per-document service guarantee, and older queued work may be overtaken by due delayed jobs or repeated dispatch. Recursive split children become `pending` and are selected by source offset within their own document, not given a global priority. Current HEAD caps **whole-document** split parents at root count × 2, checked under its Postgres row lock; reaching the budget marks the failing chunk `split_limit` and preserves partial coverage. This limits amplification but does not allocate fair worker time among documents. No WO lock serializes an entire document: merge's lock is per merge unit, and legacy stage/summary locks are per class and document. A single merge or summary still occupies one shared extraction process for up to 330/200 seconds.

**Important recovery risk at scale:** `recover()` resets a chunk or merge with `status=queued` for over 10 minutes back to pending and repumps it. A queue wait over 10 minutes can trigger this even when the original Redis message is still waiting. Duplicate deliveries should be blocked by the DB claim, but queued message count and free token-count calls can grow. Visual recovery uses the same 10-minute queued threshold. A `running` chunk older than seven minutes is marked `uncertain`, conservatively avoiding an automatic second paid call. Before deliberately building a queue whose oldest age may exceed 10 minutes, distinguish a lost dispatch from a merely backed-up queue message.

## 4. Queue isolation and initial worker design

Recommend **three queues/pools** now:

| Pool | Work | Conservative first target | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| `extraction` | `ProcessDocumentChunkJob`; legacy Anthropic jobs and `GenerateInsightsJob`; `OcrPageBatchJob` when Claude OCR may run; `ProcessDocumentVisualJob`; AI comparison or another explicitly provider-gated path | Begin at **2**, then **4** after provider and resource checks; per-doc 2 | Current known capacity is 2. Four gives two documents room without one taking every worker. Higher numbers have no CPU/RAM or account-limit evidence. |
| `synthesis` | `GenerateDocumentSummaryJob` and `MergeDocumentEvidenceJob` | **2** processes, 360s worker timeout; monitor merge occupancy | Removes completion work from extraction backlog. A two-process pool allows one long merge and one summary concurrently. Merge can call Anthropic for optional context and is DB heavy. Separate `merge` queue/pool only if two concurrent merges delay synthesis materially. |
| `default` | Scan, text extraction, visual planning, Voyage embeddings, mail, reminders, billing and other short work | **2** processes to start, **at least 150s** worker timeout if 120s jobs move here; 128 MB PHP worker limit must be profiled for parser/rasterization | Keeps user-visible scanning and billing independent of Anthropic extraction. Consider a separate CPU/IO pool only if large file parsing or rasterization consumes both general workers. |

An initial 2+2+2 is **six PHP processes versus today's maximum five** (3 default + 2 extraction); it is a topology change before a major concurrency increase. A 4+2+2 target is eight and requires measured container RAM/CPU, DB connection and account headroom. Configure explicit `minProcesses` and `maxProcesses` per pool; remember a minimum across several services is a standing resource commitment. A dedicated merge pool now adds process overhead and operational complexity without evidence of sustained merge volume. If the real requirement is a hard bound on merge DB/CPU load, add a one-process merge pool later rather than hoping queue ordering provides it.

Dispatch sites must be changed together: changing a job class alone does not reroute chained, batched, callback, recovery, or self-retry dispatches. Scan stays default. Text extraction, `AnalyzeEmbeddedVisualsJob`, and `GenerateEmbeddingsJob` should move to default **only after** the general worker timeout/memory is made suitable. `GenerateInsightsJob` calls Anthropic and belongs with provider-gated work, despite being listed among general candidates. OCR and comparison are conditional Anthropic callers and need the same global gate regardless of queue. No job is currently class-unique; preserve DB checkpoints and WO keys through rerouting.

## 5. Two independent concurrency limits and provider safety

**Per-document:** retain `DOCINTEL_EXTRACTION_CONCURRENCY=2` initially. Its Postgres row-lock dispatch window is simple and race safe for incremental chunks, but a queued job consumes a document slot even while waiting. Increase to 3 only after global capacity is at least 6, fairness is observed, and budget/DB lock behavior is measured. Use a document-scoped active lease only if measured queue fairness requires it; do not replace durable chunk claims with a Redis-only counter.

**Global:** the existing two-process extraction supervisor is only a per-replica **job** cap; it is not a global Anthropic call cap. `AnthropicClient` has a cache counter at 40 requests/minute, with non-waiting behavior for incremental extraction/summary and waiting behavior for many other calls. The increment/first-key-put sequence can race, has no simultaneous-call bound, and does not cover token counting in the same way. It also does not enforce Anthropic input/output token-per-minute limits. Increasing the worker count to 6, 8, or 12 could exceed the account's unknown request or token limits and amplify 429s. Synchronous web Q&A and optional merge context calls bypass Horizon extraction capacity.

Implement one **central Redis lease/semaphore at the Anthropic request boundary** for all message, count-token, OCR, vision, merge-context, comparison, and web Q&A paths. Use atomic acquire/release (Redis Lua or a proven shared semaphore), unique owner tokens, lease expiry longer than the longest provider request, and renewal if necessary. Release in `finally`; reject or defer without sleeping in a worker when full. A permit should bound *in-flight provider HTTP calls*, including each retry/repair request, rather than all CPU work in a job. Set the ceiling from account limits and monitor both active leases and actual calls. Queue middleware can be an optimization for provider-only jobs, but it misses calls inside merge and the web path; `RateLimited` or `WithoutOverlapping` release also consumes attempts unless `retryUntil`/attempt policy is adjusted. A Redis semaphore plus the existing Postgres chunk claim addresses separate concerns: global call capacity versus per-document durable idempotency. With multiple worker replicas, sum supervisor process counts for Railway sizing, but let the single shared semaphore enforce the provider cap.

429 handling is partly present: failures classify 429/5xx as transient; typed incremental requests have one transport attempt, bounded explicit chunk/summary redispatches, exponential delay and jitter, and `Retry-After` parsing. Legacy calls may retry in-process up to four times and sleep up to 30 seconds; job retry can multiply this. `Retry-After` is parsed as an integer (a date-valued header would become zero), and provider-specific rate-limit headers are not used for adaptive admission. Extraction timeout is 120s, chunk job 180s; synthesis uses 110/90/75/60s levels plus bounded repair within a 200s job. A timeout after the provider accepted work is financially ambiguous: a conservative `uncertain` checkpoint should require review, not automatic replay. The DB claim blocks concurrent duplicate paid chunk calls; there remain crash windows after a provider response and before durable result persistence, so exactly-once provider billing is impossible without provider idempotency support. Existing cost reservation/settlement and one-debit credit ledger must remain intact.

Ramp **2 → 4 → 6 → 8** global provider permits and extraction capacity in separate, observed steps, never simply by changing `maxProcesses` on multiple replicas. At each step record p95 provider latency, account 429s, tokens/minute versus account limits, timeout/unknown-usage rate, actual active calls, job retries, oldest queue age, per-document time, worker CPU/RSS, Redis memory, DB connections/query latency, and spend. Roll back a step when 429s persist, timeouts/unknown spend rise, or queue improvement disappears. Account limits and available CPU/RAM are unknown in this audit; no claim that 8 is safe is made.

## 6. Region, Railway, Redis and Postgres findings

Railway production metadata at audit time reports `CA_BACKEND` and `ca-horizon-worker` as one replica each in `us-west2`, Postgres and Redis as one replica each in `sfo`. Web and worker both run the audited commit. **Do not perform a region move based on the historical Virginia diagram.** Measure app↔DB/Redis round-trip and check Railway's exact region mapping. If a future mismatch is confirmed, move stateless web/workers toward Postgres/Redis, not the stateful services. All four services have volumes: Postgres `/var/lib/postgresql`, Redis `/data`, and separate web/worker ClamAV signature volumes `/var/lib/clamav` (5 GB allocations, about 0.25–0.5 GB used). A move/replacement must recreate or migrate ClamAV signatures and pass startup scanner checks. Postgres and Redis volumes make their relocation riskier; leave them in place for this plan.

Existing private Railway reference-variable names may remain stable when stateless services change region, but DNS resolution, route, connection errors and latency must be verified; no URL or secret value needs to be copied into this audit. A region move or service split rebuilds/restarts instances and may interrupt an in-flight job. Drain Horizon first, allow the 390s Redis visibility window, verify checkpoints/queued dispatches, then start new workers; use the normal deployment process with a rollback route. Do not assume a volume can be attached to multiple replicas: web/worker each have a separate ClamAV volume today, and replica/service placement rules need Railway confirmation. Horizontal worker scaling also increases DB connections, Redis clients, Horizon metadata and CPU/RAM. Large PDF parsing/rasterization, ClamAV scan and vector writes have a materially different resource profile from network-bound Anthropic calls. `pulse_*` maintenance and queue/DB metadata add write load. No production CPU/RAM chart, Redis memory trend, DB connection count, or query latency was accessible here, so the worker counts above are guardrails to validate, not capacity estimates.

## 7. Scaling stages and decision thresholds

These are *simultaneous-user* planning bands, not promises of documents/hour. One user may upload multiple documents or perform Q&A. The ceilings below assume a **shared Redis provider semaphore** and that account/request/token limits permit the ramp. Keep the three queues throughout; add services or a merge pool only from measurements.

| Stage | Extraction / synthesis / default processes (total across replicas) | Per-document chunks / global provider calls | Railway services | Advance or intervene when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: beta, 1–10 users | Start **2 / 2 / 2**; test **4 / 2 / 2** | 2 / 2, then 4 if safe | Existing web + one Horizon worker + Postgres + Redis (4) | p95 oldest extraction wait repeatedly > 2 min, synthesis wait > 1 min, or scan wait > 30s triggers investigation; 429s and timeouts should be near zero. |
| B: 10–50 users | **4–8 / 2 / 2–3**, raised in steps | 2 / 4–8, account-limited | Prefer same 4 services until worker CPU/RAM demands dedicated replicas; if split, web + extraction worker + synthesis/general worker + DB + Redis (5) | Require sustained queue drain, measured per-document fairness, DB connection headroom, Redis memory stability, and no rising 429/unknown-cost trend. |
| C: 50–200 users | Capacity-derived, initially model **8–16 / 2–4 / 3–6** as an *experiment range*, not an approved setting | 2 (possibly 3 after fairness test) / account-derived shared cap, **not** sum of replicas | Split worker roles into 2–3 Railway services when isolation/operations justify it; total 5–6 with web, DB, Redis | Use measured arrivals, service times and p95 oldest age to size. Introduce fair document admission and possibly independent merge/CPU work if tails persist; account rate/token limits may force lower global concurrency. |

The 20-document scenario exposes a fairness requirement even at larger global capacity: per-doc=2 and global=8 allows only four documents to have two active calls at once. Queue FIFO gives eventual progress under finite load, not bounded progress under continuous arrivals. If p95 per-document first-chunk wait is poor, dispatch one ready chunk per document in round-robin order, then allow each document's second slot when other waiting documents have received one. Preserve Postgres checkpoints and a durable ready-document set; never use Redis leases as the sole source of document state. Apply upload/admission backpressure or a user-facing queued state if arrival rate remains above measured drain rate.

## 9. Failure isolation, exact change surfaces and tests

Queue names are transport routing, not batch identity. Laravel batches/chains use shared `job_batches`/Redis/DB state and will complete across queues when every named queue has an active worker. The existing upload chain explicitly sets child queues; the legacy AI batch sets its queue; its `finally` callback may dispatch summary to `synthesis` safely. The incremental `pump` may dispatch merge or summary to another queue after commit. A synthesis job dispatched by an extraction worker can safely land on another supervisor. The **risk is a missed dispatch site or absent listener**, leaving a stage queued forever; verify all paths including fallback, re-analysis, delayed retry and resume. A worker restart preserves durable DB chunks, summaries, processing stages, OCR results and credit ledger; an in-flight request remains ambiguous and should follow the current uncertain/review policy. Re-routing does not itself establish stronger idempotency.

6. Saturate extraction workers and prove synthesis and scan/general jobs complete on their own supervisors; include a long merge occupancy case.
7. Duplicate queued/running/completed chunk delivery produces at most one billable extraction request and preserves the result; retries never bypass either limit. Check the pre-claim token-count side effect.
8. A >10-minute legitimate queue wait does not cause unbounded redispatch; a genuinely lost dispatch is recovered once; interrupted paid calls become uncertain, not automatically replayed.
9. Re-analysis, legacy batch `finally`, chains, OCR page checkpoints, visual recovery and cross-queue serialization/deserialization preserve queue assignment and completion.
10. Credit reservation/settlement and billing webhook replay remain one-debit/idempotent under duplicate delivery and cross-queue execution. Keep 390s visibility > every job/worker timeout after rerouting.
11. Simulate 429 with numeric/date `Retry-After`, timeout, repair, and missing rate headers; verify bounded retry and no real Anthropic calls.
12. Run a multi-document queue fairness test with 5/10/20 documents and delayed/split work; assert bounded first-progress wait under the chosen scheduler policy rather than inventing throughput.

Existing tests already cover completed-chunk redelivery (`IncrementalDocumentPipelineTest`), duplicate synthesis billing and fallback (`SynthesisFallbackAndThroughputTest`), merge idempotency and timeout ordering (`DocumentRoutingAndMergeScaleTest`), and transaction recovery (`DocumentJobTransactionRecoveryTest`). Current HEAD adds `CostGuardrailsTest`, which checks the **already implemented** document-wide split budget and repeat routing of the same document, plus usage-report and model-routing tests. These are useful regression bases but do not prove multi-worker fairness or a cross-replica global provider cap. Do not duplicate the split-budget implementation or its basic test.

## 10. Safe deployment sequence and priorities

1. **P0 — Baseline and guardrails.** Confirm production effective queue/cache driver, provider request/token limits, Railway CPU/RAM, Postgres connection headroom, Redis memory and migration status privately; add per-queue age/depth and provider 429/active-call metrics. Investigate the 10-minute queued recovery threshold before letting waits approach it. Confirm scheduler actually runs `docintel:resume` and any Horizon snapshots. No move is indicated by current region metadata.
2. **P0 — Make routing complete.** Implement the three queues and all dispatch-path changes together, with listeners and timeouts sized before moving 90–120s jobs to default. Add the central atomic provider gate covering web and merge paths. Keep extraction at 2 until these checks pass; validate with fake-provider tests and a no-provider staging load test.
3. **P1 — Controlled rollout.** Deploy code/config with all queues listened to before dispatching new queue names; drain/restart workers gracefully under the normal release process. Verify batch/chain completion, pending/failed job counts, scan and synthesis wait, checkpoints, one-debit billing and 390s visibility. Then try 4 extraction/global calls if account and resource metrics allow. Change one capacity dimension at a time; retain rollback to the previous queue listeners until old messages drain.
4. **P1 — Fairness and service sizing.** Use measured first-chunk wait and oldest age for 5–20 documents. Add round-robin document admission/backpressure if large documents still dominate; separate worker roles/replicas only after checking ClamAV volume and total process multiplication. Add merge pool only if measured merge occupancy starves synthesis.
5. **P2 — Further scale.** Adaptive provider rate/token budgeting from account headers, per-model quotas, autoscaling based on queue age plus CPU/RAM, and dedicated CPU-heavy OCR/merge service if workload warrants. Revisit regional placement only if actual cross-region RTT or Railway placement changes.

**Remaining unknowns:** account RPM/ITPM/OTPM and model-specific limits; actual request/repair/token-count mix; current uploads/hour and document size distribution; production p50/p95 queue and provider latencies; Railway CPU/RAM and plan limits; Postgres/Redis operational metrics; whether production env overrides defaults; migration status (SSH attempt did not return); scheduler deployment; whether semantic KPI matching is enabled; current OCR provider mix; and whether Railway volume/region constraints permit additional worker replicas. These determine safe numerical capacity. They should be measured before treating any Stage B/C process range as a target.
